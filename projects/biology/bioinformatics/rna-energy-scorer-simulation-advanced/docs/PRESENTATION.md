# Presentation Guide — RNA Folding Free-Energy Scorer: Simulation (Logistic Growth)

## 60-Second Pitch

A RNA Folding Free-Energy Scorer simulation in pure Python that integrates logistic growth with Euler steps and prints the full trajectory toward carrying capacity. RNA folding free energy sums stacking energies of adjacent base pairs plus loop penalties; lower energy means more stable structure.

RNA Folding Free-Energy Scorer in two sentences: RNA folding free energy sums stacking energies of adjacent base pairs plus loop penalties; lower energy means more stable structure. A simplified scorer evaluates a given structure with a small thermodynamic parameter set instead of full dynamic programming. It ranks candidate structures by stability.

## Architecture Talking Points

- Components: a single module plus the CLI entrypoint — each maps to a real module in `src/`, and the data-flow diagram shows how they connect.
- Algorithm: Explicit Euler integration of logistic growth; runs in O(steps).
- Honest scaling limit: The trajectory is a plain list, so millions of steps would use noticeable memory; streaming or downsampling would fix that.

Use these when someone asks "how does it work?" — start with the problem, then walk the data flow, then name one trade-off. Trade-offs are what interviewers remember.

## Interview Q&A

**Q:** What dynamics does the model show?

**A:** Logistic growth: fast early rise that levels off at the carrying capacity K, a common shape for adoption and saturation processes.

**Q:** How is accuracy controlled?

**A:** --steps and --dt; smaller dt is more accurate, larger dt runs faster but can overshoot.

**Q:** Why Euler instead of RK4?

**A:** Euler is first-order and simple to read; the template favors clarity over the accuracy of higher-order integrators.
